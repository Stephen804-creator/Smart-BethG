**Scope:** The nine external setup/acquisition workstreams needed to prepare Smart BethG for a responsible commercial launch, plus the owner's question about AI-assisted artwork and icons.

> This is an execution guide, not legal, tax, accounting, security-certification, or accessibility-certification advice. Requirements and prices can change. Confirm obligations with the relevant Nigerian authority, provider, and qualified professional before paying or representing the product as compliant.

## How to work through this guide

Complete these workstreams sequentially, not all at once. The recommended first external action is to obtain legal advice on the correct business structure and obligations before registering an entity. Low-cost research and evidence collection can begin now. Do not buy services, register for obligations, or commission expensive audits merely to tick a box.

For every item, record:
- **Status:** Not started / Researching / Waiting for advice or provider / In progress / Evidence collected / Reviewed / Complete / Not applicable (reason recorded).
- **Owner action:** What the owner must do.
- **Evidence to retain:** Documents, receipts, settings exports, test reports, or written decisions.
- **Review outcome:** What remains unresolved and who must resolve it.

A checklist is not complete just because an account exists or a document has been drafted. Completion means the relevant action has been performed, evidence is retained, and any material legal or technical decision has been reviewed.

## 1. Business registration and structure

**Purpose:** Decide who legally operates Smart BethG, owns its intellectual property, signs provider contracts, receives payments, and carries responsibility to users.

### Procedure
1. First discuss the proposed business with a Nigerian technology/commercial lawyer or qualified business adviser.
2. Explain that Smart BethG is a software/AI platform, may process customer data, may offer paid plans, and may later expand into other products or jurisdictions.
3. Ask whether to begin as an individual/sole proprietor using a registered business name, or incorporate a limited liability company now. Ask about tax, liability, reporting, ownership, funding, contracts, and the cost of changing structure later.
4. Do not assume a business name and a limited liability company provide the same liability protection.
5. Once the structure is decided, use the official Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) Corporate Registry Portal: https://icrp.cac.gov.ng/ . Check the current CAC procedure and fees at https://cac.gov.ng/ before payment.
6. Complete name reservation and the relevant registration form; provide accurate identity, address, ownership and business activity details; pay only through the official portal/payment route.
7. Download and securely retain the certificate, status report/certified extract where applicable, application details, and payment receipts.
8. Keep the registered details consistent across payment providers, bank accounts, contracts, invoices, domain ownership and public business disclosures.

### Evidence required
- Written advice explaining the recommended structure and why.
- CAC name reservation and registration confirmation.
- Registration certificate and current company/business details.
- Official payment receipts and a record of statutory filing/reporting dates.
- A decision on who owns existing code, brand assets, domains, accounts and contracts.

**Important future-transition issue:** If Smart BethG begins under an individual and later moves into a company, plan the transfer/assignment or licensing of intellectual property, domains, provider accounts, contracts, customer relationships and data-controller responsibilities. Do not assume that incorporating a company automatically transfers these assets or agreements.

**Official starting points:** CAC portal https://icrp.cac.gov.ng/ ; CAC services https://cac.gov.ng/services/business-name .

## 2. Legal services and professional advice

**Purpose:** Obtain advice tailored to Smart BethG's real business model and actual data/AI practices, not generic policy templates.

### Procedure
1. Identify at least three Nigerian lawyers or law firms with demonstrable experience in technology, privacy/data protection, software/IP licensing, and commercial contracts.
2. Verify the lawyer's professional standing and the firm's identity independently. Do not rely solely on social-media advertisements.
3. Request a fixed-fee initial consultation or a clearly scoped written quotation. Explain that the project is being built on a limited budget and ask what must be handled before launch versus later.
4. Send a concise description of the actual product: user accounts, conversations, memory/personalization, AI providers, file uploads, connected services, payment plans, customer support, user data export, and the proposed model-improvement/training contribution.
5. Ask for written advice on:
   - suitable business structure and founder-to-company IP ownership;
   - applicable Nigerian and foreign laws based on users, operations, and providers;
   - NDPA/NDPC roles, registration/classification and DPIA obligations;
   - whether training contribution may be default-on, what notice and consent are required, and which categories must be excluded;
   - privacy notice, terms, acceptable-use rules, refund/cancellation terms and complaint handling;
   - provider, processor, contractor, employee and customer agreements;
   - intellectual-property ownership and third-party licence obligations;
   - consumer protection, tax, marketing claims and sector-specific restrictions.
6. Ask the lawyer to distinguish mandatory law from recommended risk controls and assumptions requiring further facts.
7. Retain the engagement letter, invoice/receipt, advice, assumptions, and follow-up actions.

### Suggested first-contact message

> Hello. I am developing Smart BethG, an AI-enabled software platform intended for commercial use. I need a scoped legal consultation covering Nigerian business structure, technology and data-protection obligations, AI data use/model-improvement consent, intellectual-property ownership and third-party licences, customer terms/refunds, and future transfer from an individual founder to a company if appropriate. Please let me know your relevant experience, consultation fee, availability, and whether you can provide written advice and a prioritized pre-launch action list.

### Evidence required
- Shortlist and professional verification notes.
- Engagement letter and fee/receipt.
- Written legal advice with jurisdiction, assumptions, and priority actions.
- A tracked list of unresolved questions and agreed next review date.

## 3. Data-protection assessment (NDPA / NDPC)

**Purpose:** Determine the actual obligations that apply to Smart BethG's processing of personal data and implement them before public launch.

### Procedure
1. Prepare a data inventory before paying for a registration or audit. For each data category, record what is collected, why, where it comes from, where it is stored, who can access it, how long it is retained, and how it is deleted.
2. Include account and contact data, authentication/security logs, conversations, prompts, uploaded files, generated outputs, memory/personalization, support records, billing metadata, analytics, connected-app data, and any proposed training/model-improvement dataset.
3. Map each processor/provider, its role, processing location, subprocessors, retention/deletion terms, and any international data transfer.
4. Estimate the number and type of Nigerian data subjects and identify sensitive/high-risk processing, children, profiling, large-scale monitoring, and other factors relevant to the law and current NDPC instruments.
5. Ask a currently NDPC-licensed Data Protection Compliance Organisation (DPCO) for a scoped assessment. Verify its current licence on the NDPC website before engagement: https://ndpc.gov.ng/licensed-dpcos/ .
6. Ask the DPCO to determine in writing whether Smart BethG is a data controller, processor, or both for each activity; whether it qualifies as a Data Controller or Processor of Major Importance; whether registration, compliance audit returns, a Data Protection Officer, DPIA, policies, or other measures are required.
7. Ask the DPCO to identify the legal basis and transparency/consent requirements for each purpose separately. Do not treat consent to use the app, memory, analytics, user export, and model training as one interchangeable permission.
8. If a DPIA is required or appropriate, complete it before the high-risk processing begins and implement its mitigations.
9. Implement data-subject request handling: access, correction, deletion, objection/withdrawal where applicable, portability/export where applicable, complaint escalation, and identity verification.
10. Keep the assessment current when user numbers, product features, providers, countries, data types, or processing purposes materially change.

### Evidence required
- Data inventory and data-flow map.
- Provider/subprocessor inventory and transfer map.
- Written DPCO classification and obligation assessment.
- DPCO licence verification and engagement evidence.
- DPIA and mitigation records, if required.
- NDPC registration/audit-return evidence, if applicable.
- Implemented and tested data-subject request procedures.

**Important:** Do not conclude that registration is unnecessary solely because the service is small or has few users. Apply the current NDPA and NDPC instruments to the actual activities and classification criteria. Obtain a written determination.

## 4. Domain and business email

**Purpose:** Establish durable, owner-controlled contact channels and protect the identity of the service.

### Procedure
1. Choose a domain that is easy to spell, pronounce, and associate with Smart BethG. Check spelling, confusingly similar names, trademark risks, and renewal cost.
2. Compare the first-year price with the renewal price, DNS control, transfer rules, account recovery, privacy options, and support.
3. If choosing a .ng domain, use a NiRA-accredited registrar listed by the Nigeria Internet Registration Association: https://www.nira.org.ng/ .
4. Register the domain in the correct owner's legal name and an email address the owner controls. Enable multi-factor authentication and registrar/domain-lock protections where available.
5. Enable auto-renew only after confirming payment method and renewal notices; keep a separate reminder and recovery details.
6. Create role-based contact addresses or aliases where practical, such as support@, privacy@, legal@, and security@. These can initially route to one monitored inbox if the provider supports it.
7. Configure SPF and DKIM for authorized sending and publish a carefully configured DMARC policy. Start with monitoring/reporting before moving to enforcement if necessary.
8. Test sending, receiving, password recovery, and account access from a second device. Document who can access each mailbox.

### Evidence required
- Domain registrar, registrant/owner details, expiry and renewal dates.
- Receipt and account recovery/MFA evidence (do not store passwords in this document).
- DNS record export or screenshots for SPF, DKIM and DMARC.
- Successful mail-delivery test and monitored contact inboxes.

## 5. Payment provider

**Purpose:** Choose a payment service that legally and operationally supports the business structure, product category, customers and payout arrangement.

### Procedure
1. Compare providers available to the actual business and country, such as Paystack and Flutterwave. Review current supported business types, fees, settlement timing, currencies, chargebacks, refunds, reserves, prohibited/restricted activities and support.
2. Ask each provider which account type is appropriate now: individual/unregistered starter account, registered business, or incorporated company. Do not misrepresent the business status.
3. Confirm that the provider supports the exact Smart BethG services and subscription/payment model. Ask about recurring billing, international cards, invoices, taxes, disputes, refunds, webhook security and payout requirements.
4. Review the current provider terms, privacy notice, acceptable-use policy, data-processing terms and any marketplace/platform restrictions.
5. Create a test-mode account first. Build and test successful payment, declined payment, cancellation, refund, duplicate webhook, delayed webhook, dispute, subscription renewal and failed-renewal flows.
6. Implement server-side verification of payment status. Never rely only on a browser redirect or client-side success message.
7. Verify webhook signatures, make webhook processing idempotent, log safe audit events, and reconcile provider transactions against internal orders/subscriptions.
8. Submit accurate KYC documents only when ready. Ensure names, bank details, registration information and identity details match.
9. Activate live payments only after legal terms, pricing, refunds, support, security and reconciliation are ready.

### Evidence required
- Provider comparison and written confirmation of suitable account type/product category.
- Current provider terms and fee schedule saved with review date.
- KYC submission/approval and payout account confirmation.
- Test-mode test results for success, failure, refunds, disputes and webhooks.
- Live-mode approval and controlled low-value end-to-end test when authorized.

**Provider starting points:** Paystack https://paystack.com/ ; Flutterwave https://flutterwave.com/ . Requirements and available account types can change; confirm directly before relying on a previous checklist.

## 6. Provider and processor agreements

**Purpose:** Know what each third party may do with Smart BethG data, content, files and customer information.

### Procedure
1. Build a complete provider register: AI/model providers, hosting, database, storage, email, payment, analytics, error monitoring, customer support, authentication, search, automation, CDN, backups and any connected-app integration.
2. For each provider, record product/service name, account owner, purpose, data categories sent, processing region, subprocessors, retention, deletion method, security controls, contract URL/version/date and renewal/cancellation process.
3. Read the applicable Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, Data Processing Addendum (DPA), acceptable-use rules, API terms, model terms and commercial-use/licensing terms.
4. Specifically check whether the provider uses prompts, files, conversations, outputs or metadata to train/improve its models; whether that use can be disabled; whether the setting applies to API and consumer accounts equally; and what exceptions apply.
5. Check retention, deletion, backup retention, breach notification, audit evidence, access controls, international transfers, subprocessors, service termination, data export and support obligations.
6. Check whether provider terms permit commercial use and the intended redistribution, embedding, customer-facing generation or model deployment.
7. Ask for a DPA or written contractual clarification where required. Do not rely on a marketing page if the binding contract says something different.
8. Save a dated copy or stable link to the applicable agreement and record the account-level privacy/training settings.
9. Re-review when a provider changes terms, model, product, region, account type or processing purpose.

### Evidence required
- Complete provider/subprocessor register.
- Dated terms, DPAs and relevant contract copies.
- Per-provider data-use/training decision and account setting evidence.
- Written answers for unresolved retention, deletion, location, transfer or commercial-use questions.
- Review date and responsible owner.

## 7. Asset, software, model and content licences

**Purpose:** Ensure that code, fonts, icons, illustrations, images, templates, datasets, models, audio/video and other materials can be used in the intended commercial product.

### Procedure
1. Inventory every third-party or externally sourced asset used in the product, website, documentation, marketing, generated content and build pipeline.
2. For each item, record its name, source URL, author/rightsholder, version, exact licence, date obtained, intended use, modifications, attribution requirements, commercial-use permission, redistribution rules and evidence of purchase/permission.
3. For open-source software, inspect the actual licence and dependencies. Check obligations for notices, source-code disclosure, modifications, distribution, linking, network use, patent grants and compatible licences. Do not reject a licence merely because it is GPL; determine whether its obligations can be met for the actual distribution model.
4. For fonts, images, icons, illustrations, stock assets and templates, check whether the licence covers commercial use, SaaS, embedding, modification, redistribution, client deliverables, number of users and use in a logo/brand. A licence to display an image on one website may not permit embedding it in a product customers can extract.
5. For AI models and datasets, inspect model cards, dataset terms, weights/code licences, training-data restrictions, acceptable-use terms, attribution and commercial deployment rights separately.
6. For user-submitted content, define ownership/licence permissions in user terms and avoid assuming that upload grants unlimited training or marketing rights.
7. Retain invoices, receipts, licence text, attribution files, permission emails and the exact version/hash where practical.
8. Run dependency and asset scans before release; generate and maintain a Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) for software dependencies.
9. Flag unclear, non-commercial, personal-use-only, unknown-origin, or incompatible material for replacement or written permission. Do not ship it until resolved.
10. Treat assets produced with an AI assistant as a separate record: retain the original source, editing history, prompt/context where useful, and the final version; see Section 10 below.

### Evidence required
- Asset and dependency register.
- Licence/permission copies and receipts.
- Required attribution and notices in the repository/product.
- SBOM and dependency/licence scan results.
- Written resolution for every restricted or unclear item.

## 8. Independent security testing

**Purpose:** Obtain evidence that the deployed application and its critical trust boundaries resist realistic attacks.

### Procedure
1. Finish internal security work first: authentication, authorization, tenant isolation, secure sessions, secret management, input validation, upload handling, webhook verification, rate limits, security headers, database/storage permissions, backups and recovery.
2. Create a staging environment representative of production. Use synthetic test data, not real customer data. Keep staging isolated from production credentials and services.
3. Write a test scope covering the actual Smart BethG architecture and endpoints. Include:
   - registration, login, password reset, email verification, sessions and account recovery;
   - role and admin access, object-level authorization, tenant/user isolation and IDOR;
   - APIs, input validation, injection, XSS, CSRF and security headers;
   - file uploads, archives, path traversal, downloads and storage access;
   - AI prompts, tools, connected services, provider keys and data leakage;
   - background workers, job queues, cancellation, retries and resource exhaustion;
   - any code execution, terminal, browser or sandbox feature and its network/filesystem boundaries;
   - webhooks, payment callbacks, rate limiting, logging, backup and recovery.
4. Obtain written authorization, scope, rules of engagement, test window, allowed techniques, source/IP restrictions, emergency contact and data-handling requirements before testing.
5. Request at least three quotations from qualified application-security testers or firms. Verify relevant web/API/cloud experience, sample report quality, confidentiality and independence.
6. Use a recognized methodology such as OWASP Web Security Testing Guide. Ask for findings with severity, affected endpoint, reproducible evidence, impact, remediation and retest status.
7. Fix critical/high findings before launch; document accepted residual risks and obtain an explicit owner/legal decision for any unresolved issue.
8. Commission a retest of fixes. Run automated dependency, secret, container and static analysis checks as appropriate.
9. Repeat testing after major architecture, authentication, payment, file-handling, AI-tool or infrastructure changes.

### Evidence required
- Written authorization and test scope.
- Staging environment description and data-safety confirmation.
- Tester identity/qualifications and engagement agreement.
- Final report, findings register, remediation commits and retest report.
- Evidence that critical/high issues are resolved or launch remains blocked.
- Actual-host security acceptance record.

**Methodology:** OWASP Web Security Testing Guide https://owasp.org/www-project-web-security-testing-guide/ .

## 9. Accessibility assessment

**Purpose:** Make Smart BethG usable by people with different abilities, devices, input methods and assistive technologies.

### Procedure
1. Adopt WCAG 2.2 Level AA as the product target unless a legal or customer requirement calls for more.
2. List critical user journeys: registration, login, onboarding, navigation, chat, file upload/download, settings, billing, cancellation, support, privacy controls and data export.
3. Run automated accessibility checks on representative pages, but do not treat an automated scan as proof of accessibility.
4. Test every critical journey using keyboard only. Confirm visible focus, logical focus order, no keyboard traps, operable dialogs/menus and accessible error recovery.
5. Check text and UI contrast, text resizing, zoom/reflow, responsive layouts, meaningful headings, labels, accessible names, form instructions, validation messages, alt text and status announcements.
6. Test with screen readers on representative desktop and mobile combinations. Include dynamic chat updates, loading states, generated content, menus and modals.
7. Check that icons are not the only way to communicate meaning and that interactive controls have accessible names and adequate target sizes.
8. Commission an independent accessibility review before launch if budget permits. Give the assessor the critical journeys and ask for reproducible findings and retest.
9. Fix barriers, retest, and add accessibility checks to the normal design and release process.

### Evidence required
- WCAG target and critical-journey list.
- Automated scan output and manual test notes.
- Keyboard and screen-reader results.
- Contrast measurements and responsive/reflow results.
- Independent assessment and remediation/retest evidence where commissioned.

**Reference:** W3C WCAG 2.2 Quick Reference https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/ .

## 10. AI-assisted artwork, icons and other creative assets

**Purpose:** Establish a defensible, documented process for artwork that the owner creates and then edits or improves with ChatGPT or another AI tool.

### Owner's current example

The owner describes drawing an icon in an HTML editor, then bringing that icon to ChatGPT for a small edit because the owner did not know how to create the desired interactive detail. The final icon is intended for possible commercial use in Smart BethG.

### Practical answer

Using ChatGPT to assist with a small edit does **not, by itself, make the icon prohibited for commercial use**. Under OpenAI's Terms of Use, as between the user and OpenAI and to the extent permitted by applicable law, the user retains rights in their input and owns the output; OpenAI assigns any rights it may have in that output to the user. The user remains responsible for ensuring they had rights to submit the input and that the resulting content does not infringe another party's rights. Output may not be unique, and similar output may be produced for other users.

This means:
- The original icon/drawing/code the owner created remains the owner's input, assuming it was original and did not copy protected material.
- The owner may use the ChatGPT-assisted result commercially subject to the applicable OpenAI terms, other applicable laws, and third-party rights.
- ChatGPT assistance does not automatically transfer the owner's original work to OpenAI or impose a separate third-party asset licence merely because ChatGPT helped edit it.
- If the owner supplied a third-party icon, screenshot, font, logo, template or other protected material, the owner still needs permission or a licence for that input and for the intended use.
- If the AI change is largely generated by the model, the extent to which the AI-generated portion itself receives copyright protection is a separate legal question. The owner's original human-created expression and meaningful human creative modifications/selection may remain protectable; do not assume every AI-generated detail is independently copyrightable.
- Commercial permission under a service's terms and copyright ownership/enforceability are different questions. Permission to use an output does not guarantee exclusive rights over it or guarantee that it is non-infringing.

### Procedure for every AI-assisted asset
1. Save the original human-created file before AI editing (for example, the original HTML, CSS, SVG or image).
2. Save the AI-assisted version as a separate file. Do not overwrite the only original.
3. Record the tool used, approximate date, purpose of the edit, and a short description of what the owner created versus what the AI changed.
4. Confirm that the original input was the owner's work or that the owner had permission/licence to submit it to the AI service.
5. Inspect the final asset for copied logos, brand marks, recognizable third-party artwork, stock elements, watermarks, or suspicious similarity to a known icon set.
6. If the design resembles a known product/company logo or a distinctive commercial icon, compare it against the source and obtain legal review or redesign it before commercial release.
7. Check the current terms for the actual AI product/account used. Do not assume that terms for ChatGPT consumer accounts, the API, enterprise services, image-generation tools or other third-party tools are identical.
8. Retain the original file, final file, available edit history, relevant conversation/export, and a screenshot or note showing the design process.
9. Record the asset in the asset register with status: human-created; AI-assisted human work; predominantly AI-generated; third-party licensed; or unclear.
10. For brand marks, logos, core product identity and high-value assets, obtain legal advice on copyright, trademark clearance and registrability before investing in brand rollout.

### Suggested record for the owner's described icon

- Asset name: _Icon name / component name_
- Original author/source: Owner-created in HTML editor (owner to retain original file)
- AI tool: ChatGPT
- AI contribution: Small edit to add or improve an interactive visual detail (exact change to be described from the files)
- Third-party source material: Not reported; must be confirmed
- Intended use: Commercial Smart BethG product
- Evidence: Original HTML/SVG/CSS, AI-assisted final, relevant conversation/edit history, final visual review
- Initial assessment: **Potentially suitable for commercial use; not automatically disqualified by AI assistance. Final clearance depends on the original source, exact output, current terms, similarity/third-party rights, and applicable law.**

### Important limitation

The specific icon has not been inspected in this assessment. No conclusion is being made that it is original, visually distinct, trademark-safe, or free from third-party elements. To assess the actual asset, compare the original and final files or provide both versions for review. A lawyer should review any material brand/trademark concern.

### References
- OpenAI Terms of Use: https://openai.com/policies/row-terms-of-use/ — input ownership, output ownership assignment, user responsibility, and non-uniqueness.
- Nigerian Copyright Act, 2022: https://www.copyright.gov.ng/CopyrightAct/ — applicable Nigerian copyright framework.
- Nigerian Copyright Commission FAQ: https://eregistration.copyright.gov.ng/ncc/faq — original fixed artistic works and copyright principles.
- U.S. Copyright Office, Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf — useful comparative guidance on human authorship and AI assistance; it is not Nigerian law and does not decide the owner's Nigerian rights.

## 11. Overall order of execution

Use this dependency order to avoid unnecessary spending:

1. **Legal consultation and structure decision** — determine what entity and obligations make sense.
2. **Business registration** — register only after the structure decision.
3. **Data-protection inventory and DPCO assessment** — establish obligations based on real processing.
4. **Domain and business email** — secure owner-controlled identity and contact channels.
5. **Provider and processor agreement review** — establish data, commercial-use and retention terms.
6. **Asset/software/model licence inventory** — resolve rights before public distribution.
7. **Payment provider approval and test-mode integration** — activate live payments only when ready.
8. **Accessibility assessment** — test and remediate before release.
9. **Independent security assessment** — commission against a stable staging build and remediate/retest before release.

Some research can happen in parallel, but do not spend on external audits before the relevant product scope and staging environment are stable.

## 13. Release rule

Smart BethG must not be described as fully compliant, legally cleared, security-certified, or accessibility-certified unless the relevant assessment and evidence support that exact claim. Unresolved legal obligations, high-risk data processing, third-party rights, critical/high security findings, or material accessibility barriers must remain visible as release blockers until properly resolved or formally accepted by the appropriate decision-maker.
